Exercise 1
Explain in your own words why the Waterfall method often goes wrong when building software.
Give two reasons.

Answer: Requirements can change. Making changes can be expensive since each stage must be completed before the next begins.
Problems are discovered too late in the project. Many problems may not be discovered until the end of the project when testing occurs.

Exercise 2
Pick two of the four values in the Agile Manifesto. For each one, give an example of what it could
look like in a real team.

Answer: 1. Individuals and interactions over processes and tools — People communicating and working together are more important than rigid procedures and tools. Example: It is generally better to communicate within the team during say a morning sprint rather than implementing a new quality control measure.
2. Working software over comprehensive documentation — Having software that actually works is more valuable than producing lots of documentation. Example: A team writing software and actually fixing bugs rather than writing a report about problems or bugs.

Exercise 4
Your team works in two-week sprints. In the middle of a sprint, the CEO comes in and wants a new
feature right away. What should happen, according to Scrum?

Answer: The CEO should discuss this with the Product Owner, who decides its priority in the backlog.
